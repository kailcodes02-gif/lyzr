GSI & SI Campaign · Performance & Experiment Report

What we tested, what worked, and what it cost.

Across an 11-day flight on LinkedIn, we executed a structured testing roadmap spanning brand awareness and lead generation for Lyzr's GSI & SI motion. Targeting, creative, and budget allocation were iterated in sequence to drive down delivery costs and isolate the highest-efficiency audience–creative combinations. This report documents every test, the full budget reconciliation, and the performance each layer returned.

05

Audience strategy & budget allocation

Two levers drove the efficiency gains: how budget was paced by objective, and a deliberate migration from uploaded custom lists to seniority-based native targeting once list economics proved unworkable at scale.

$ Budget pacing by objective

  • Brand awareness — $20–40 / day per ad set. Daily budgets were set against audience size, then re-weighted toward the best-performing campaigns as delivery signal matured.
  • Conversation ads — $50–70 / day per ad set. Sponsored Messaging send delivery is budget-gated: the LinkedIn delivery system scales send volume with spend, so a higher daily floor is required to grow sends and opens.
  • Reallocation discipline. Underperforming custom-list and playbook sets were paused and their budget redeployed to the audiences clearing the auction efficiently.

→ Custom → native migration

  • Custom lists ran hot. Match-rate-adjusted list sizes were small (see below), thinning the auction and inflating CPMs to $190–450.
  • Conversation ads → native, grouped in pairs at the MD-1 / MD-2 seniority tier — Group 1: Accenture (AU + Nordic) + Fujitsu; Group 2: KPMG + TCS — to build the audience scale send delivery requires.
  • Brand awareness → native by seniority, targeting MD-1 / MD-2-equivalent job titles mapped to the same GSI & SI accounts.

10

What we learned

Consolidated takeaways across audiences, creative, budget, and conversion — and the implications for the next flight.

A Audiences & targeting

  • Custom-list economics broke at small scale. Match-adjusted list sizes below ~800 members thinned the auction and pushed CPMs to $190–450 — the costliest delivery in the account.
  • Audience size is the CPM lever. The only efficient custom list (Kaushik, 7,695 members) cleared at $22 CPM against $270+ for sub-scale lists, confirming the ~5,000-member efficiency threshold.
  • Pooling unlocked efficiency. Consolidating lists into combined sets reduced delivery cost materially — the combined-native set delivered at $18 CPM, roughly 5× more efficient than small lists.
  • Native targeting maximized engagement. Attribute-based native targeting produced the strongest click rate (1.09%), at a higher CPM than pooled audiences.
  • The efficiency–engagement trade-off is now mapped. Combined-native optimizes cost and volume; native optimizes engagement; combined-custom sits at the balance point of the two.

B Creative

  • One asset anchored delivery. NTT Data-1 ("Enterprise AI, Governed") was the top click driver at 32 clicks and an efficient $20 CPM.
  • Named-account social proof outperformed. The Accenture creative led on click rate (1.63%) in the pooled test; a standalone "Why Accenture Chose Lyzr?" unit reached 3.35% — well above benchmark. Specific customer proof beats generic positioning.
  • Playbooks are effective awareness assets. The two playbook units (Agentic AI Roadmap, Agents to Production) contributed 31 combined clicks at the top of funnel, despite underperforming as standalone lead-gen.
  • Three non-performers identified. "Why GSIs Are Building on Lyzr?", "Startup Onboarding, Automated" and "How NTT Data Reduced Incidents?" returned 0 clicks and are candidates for retirement.
  • Directional read: outcome-led and named-account hooks consistently outperformed broad category messaging.

C Budget & pacing

  • Spend followed signal. Daily pacing scaled from ~$36 to ~$160 as combined sets demonstrated lower delivery costs — budget was concentrated, not flat.
  • Sub-scale sets were paused and reallocated. The playbook lead-gen sets were stopped on day 3; that budget moved to the efficient awareness audiences.
  • Cost per 1,000 reached compressed sharply with pooling — from $200+ on small lists to $33 on combined-native.

D Awareness outcomes

  • 24,449 impressions and 160 clicks at $1,032 spend — a blended 0.65% CTR and $42 CPM.
  • ~13,900 reached across the GSI & SI target accounts (additive across sets).
  • Delivery concentrated at the intended seniority (Director-led engagement) and priority accounts (Accenture, KPMG, Fujitsu), establishing a qualified warm pool for the conversion phase.

E Lead generation & messaging

  • Strong inbox penetration, limited click-through. Conversation ads achieved a ~38% open rate (peaking at 57%) against senior titles, but converted only 2 clicks across 102 opens — the gap is at the CTA, not the delivery.
  • Conversion concentrated in one segment. The single qualified lead came from Ani · Accenture · Native, reinforcing the native-targeting advantage seen in awareness.
  • Persona signal. The Ani profile out-delivered Jessica on sends, opens and the only conversion; Jessica's India-audience sets were the weakest (17–24% open).
  • Website-visit messaging underdelivered. Routing messages to the Accenture meeting page returned 32% opens and 0 clicks — the booking CTA did not convert.
  • Cost position. ~$1,086 CPL on the conversation campaign and $1,824 across lead generation. Messaging has demonstrated reach into senior inboxes; creative and CTA optimization on the converting native segment is the lever for the next phase.
