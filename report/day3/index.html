03 — Events Research

Where GSIs will be — and where we can show up

Ani asked me to look into events where GSIs and GCCs are attending, sponsoring, or speaking — so we can find the right ones for Lyzr to participate or get a panelist seat.

How I approached this: Went to the event repositories of our target GSI accounts — Accenture, Wipro, TCS, Infosys, and others — cross-referenced with their public event calendars, and shortlisted events where they had confirmed presence. Then filtered for ones where Lyzr could realistically participate, sponsor, or speak.

Key finding: About 50% of the events I found are already over — most of the big GCC and GSI events happened in January, February, March, or early April. The remaining 10+ events are the actionable window for now.

What this means: We're at the tail end of the current events calendar. The next meaningful wave will come in ~2 months when Q3 calendars start getting published. That's the right time to revisit this list and plan ahead — not reactively like this round.

05 — My Thinking

A few things I think we should talk about

Observations from week 1 — not complaints, just patterns I noticed and a suggestion on how to think about the team structure.

Inbound has dropped — and I think I know why
We went from about 84 weekly inbounds to somewhere around 40–45 today. From what I've understood, the pod model is solid for ownership — but it means that cross-cutting work like SEO, social hygiene, email conversion, and domain authority can fall through the cracks. Nobody is owning the channel layer. Each pod is doing its own thing, and the shared infrastructure isn't being maintained consistently.
We don't have a real target list
What we're calling ABM today is mostly people who've already interacted with us — downloaded a playbook, come through a partnership, or come through an inbound. That's a re-engagement list, not a target list. My suggestion: let's build a proper outbound-first target list of 400–500 accounts a week and work it systematically. Call it whatever you want — ABM or just a target list — but let's make it real and make it weekly.
A pod structure that actually works for marketing
Here's what I think could work: product marketers own specific domains — I own GSI/SI, someone owns hyperscaler partners, someone owns Architect, and so on. They set the requirements. Then there's a shared execution layer — SEO, design, email, social, events — that serves all product marketers with clear channel-level KPIs. On top of that, a general hygiene pod that owns Lyzr-wide metrics regardless of which product is being pushed.
Product marketers own
GSI/SI, hyperscalers, Architect, emerging partners — each with their own pipeline and requirements.
Shared execution layer owns
SEO, social, email, design, events — common resources with channel KPIs, serving all product marketers.
